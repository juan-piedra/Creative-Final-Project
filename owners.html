The Owners

The Douglas Hotel was Black-owned, and three people stand behind it: George Ramsey and Robert and Mabel Rowe. The course reading calls their hotel “proof that even under segregation, Black San Diegans built spaces of joy and self-determination on their own terms.”

George Ramsey

According to BlackPast, George A. Ramsey (1889–1963) was born in Pasadena, and most sources say he arrived in San Diego in 1913. By 1916 he was “managing bars, cafes, hotels, and boarding houses that catered mainly to African Americans.” In 1924, Saito writes, Ramsey and his partner Robert Rowe built the Douglas Hotel, which BlackPast calls his most significant venture. Ramsey was also a leader of the Douglas League of Black voters and a president of the San Diego Negro Taxpayers’ League, and he was hailed as the unofficial “Black Mayor of San Diego.” When he died in 1963, the San Diego Union’s obituary called him the “Mayor of San Diego’s Harlem,” the Reader reports.

Robert and Mabel Rowe

Saito writes that Robert Rowe “passed away several months after the hotel opened,” and that his widow, Mabel Rowe, “took over his work responsibilities” and lived in the hotel. The Reader adds that she lived in a suite there with her poodle and co-managed the Douglas with Ramsey. BlackPast says Ramsey married Mabel in 1927, that the marriage ended in 1939, and that she kept managing the hotel until it was sold in 1956. The sources say little about Robert Rowe himself.

Why they matter

The Black Joy reading says Black people “have always created spaces, relationships, music, art, humor, beauty, and celebration—even under conditions designed to deny them freedom and dignity.” The owners did that as business people. They built a hotel with its own nightclub, the Creole Palace, that for decades was the only downtown hotel offering quality lodging to Black travelers during segregation.
