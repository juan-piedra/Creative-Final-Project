Bessie Smith

Bessie Smith was a blues singer who performed at the Creole Palace, the nightclub inside the Douglas Hotel. The Music as Resistance resource calls her “one of the most influential blues singers of the 1920s and 1930s, whose powerful recordings addressed love, relationships, hardship, and everyday life.”

Her life

According to Britannica, Smith was born in Chattanooga, Tennessee, most likely on April 15, 1894, and died on September 26, 1937, in Clarksdale, Mississippi. During her time, she was famously called the “Empress of the Blues.” According to an essay by Paige McGinley, a performing arts professor at Washington University in St. Louis, for National Public Radio (NPR), Smith was “orphaned at age nine” and “began touring at the age of 14” with a traveling show, where she met the blues singer Ma Rainey.

At the Creole Palace

The San Diego Reader features Smith among the list of the best Black entertainers who performed at the Creole Palace and stayed at the Douglas Hotel. The caption below the image in the course resource “Black History in San Diego & East County” calls the hotel and club “the stop for Bessie Smith, Billie Holiday, the Mills Brothers, Duke Ellington.”

The blues and Black joy

The Music as Resistance resource indicates that the blues “did not always sound like a political protest song.” It continues, saying that “Simply telling the truth about Black life could itself challenge a society that wanted Black people to be invisible, stereotyped, or silent.” Britannica notes that Smith’s music expressed “the frustrations and hopes of a whole generation of Black Americans,” while McGinley described Smith’s rendition of one song as “upbeat and defiant.”

The theme of Black joy in the Black Joy resource means “not denying that pain exists, but refusing to let pain be the whole story.” Smith’s music, as described by Britannica, fits the definition because it expressed frustrations (pain) and hopes, so pain was not the whole story.
