Billie Holiday

Billie Holiday was a jazz singer who performed at the Creole Palace, the nightclub inside the Douglas Hotel. The Music as Resistance resource names her among the performers the club hosted, and the San Diego Reader’s article on the hotel is subtitled “When Lady Day sang on Market Street.”

Her life

According to Britannica, Holiday was born in Philadelphia on April 7, 1915, and died in New York City on July 17, 1959. She was 44 years old when she passed away. The same source states that Holiday was often associated with the saxophonist Lester Young, who gave her the nickname “Lady Day.”

At the Creole Palace

The Reader features Holiday among the country’s top Black entertainers of the 1930s and 1940s who performed at the Creole Palace and stayed at the Douglas Hotel. The course resource titled “Black History in San Diego & East County” notes that the club “hosted Duke Ellington, Billie Holiday, Louis Armstrong, and Bessie Smith.”

“Strange Fruit”

Holiday recorded the song “Strange Fruit” in 1939. It was based on a poem written by a Jewish high school teacher named Abel Meeropol after he saw a photograph of the 1930 lynching in Marion, Indiana. According to Britannica, Holiday started singing “Strange Fruit” at Café Society, a New York nightclub, and her label, Columbia, declined to record it “fearing the reactions of Southern record retailers and radio stations.” Instead, Holiday recorded the song for the Commodore label, and the single sold “more than one million copies.”

The Music as Resistance resource notes that the song has been called one of the first true protest songs in American popular music: “Rather than hiding behind metaphor, the song forced listeners to confront racial violence.” According to the Black Joy resource, Black joy is “resistance in an anti-Black world, sometimes political, and sometimes simply the act of living, laughing, and dreaming in a world not built to let you do any of those things.” In her work, Holiday took part in the political aspect of protest with “Strange Fruit” and performed at clubs like the Creole Palace.
